# What If — Series 1 (10 videos)

Silent minimalist stickman Shorts/Reels · 1080×1920 · 24 fps · 30–45 s each · rendered locally with the What If kit.
Profile: `~/.claude/youtube/voice.md` · workflow: `/whatif-channel` · produced 2026-10-24.

## Research (step 2), qualitative only

No competitor view data was gathered: `/yt-viral` needs `yt-dlp`, which isn't installed. What the web search did support, with the caveat that these are vendor guides and press, not audience data:
- "What if" hypotheticals are an established Shorts format. Space, physics, biology, alternate-history and societal topics recur ([Fliki](https://fliki.ai/masterclass/prompts/what-if-shorts), [Anijam](https://www.anijam.ai/blog/youtube-video-ideas/), [OutlierKit on a "¿Qué pasaría si…?" channel](https://outlierkit.com/channel/que-pasaria-777)).
- Non-verbal Shorts are named as one way to break through ([Epidemic Sound, 2026 trends](https://www.epidemicsound.com/blog/youtube-trends-2026/)). Silent, caption-led video suits sound-off viewing ([CapCut guide](https://www.capcut.com/create/silent-video-content-ai-creators-viral)). Dialogue-free animation is having a moment in film ([A.V. Club](https://www.avclub.com/dialogue-free-animation-golden-age-flow-me-robot-dreams-wallace-and-gromit), [NPR, Modern Silent Shorts](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/nx-s1-5925114)).
- I found no channel named "What If" doing silent stickman shorts.

**Why the ten could work:**
- **01:** the wrong shadow is readable in one frame, and the swap is a real twist.
- **02:** a metaphor you can watch grow; the payoff is one honest word.
- **03:** a time-stop fantasy turned into a cost, with a gut-punch reveal.
- **04:** a social anxiety everyone has, ending on self-worth rather than likes.
- **05:** the routine loop is visible from above; a beautiful payoff.
- **06:** the price board planted at the start is paid off at the end.
- **07:** an embarrassing hook that turns into collective relief.
- **08:** spectacle plus a running clock, with a funny escalation.
- **09:** an emotional mirror, with a plant (the rocket) and a payoff.
- **10:** escalating obedience, then the most unsettling turn of the batch.

## Hooks (step 5)

Five per idea were run through `hookscore.py` (full panels in `hookscores.txt`). The scorer is calibrated on spoken hooks, so third-person visual lines all land WEAK (28–55). What it does show is that "What if **you**…" premise lines score highest, so those became the premise cards. The **opening images** were chosen editorially: each is strange in frame 1 and understood within about 1 s.

| # | Premise card (score) | Opening image (chosen) | Rejected openings, and why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | What if your shadow stopped copying you? (43) | He waves; his shadow doesn't, and has turned to look at him | "His shadow waves back late" needs 2 s to read; "shadow wanted your life" spoils the twist |
| 02 | What if every lie built a wall? (35) | Brick wall up to his eyes, a brick drops into the last gap | "14 lies today" is a stat with no picture; "your lies were visible" is generic |
| 03 | What if pausing time cost you your time? (53) | A coffee cup frozen mid-fall; he plucks it out of the air | "He never hit play" is confusing cold; "everyone froze except his clock" is two ideas |

## Script evaluation and rewrites (steps 7–8)

These are editorial notes. The weakest beat in each draft was rewritten before the shot lists below:
- **01:** the middle (an office) repeated the bus stop. Cut it, and kept the chase to the corner, which leads straight into the swap.
- **02:** five lie beats in a row felt like a list. Cut to four, each faster (2 s), so the wall's rise does the escalating.
- **03:** the montage lacked an aging cue. Added an AGE label that ticks, with posture getting worse each pause.
- **04:** the café beat had no turn. The crush's number now **rises** when he's clumsy, which plants the idea that the numbers aren't about performance.
- **05:** the middle (other people's paths) was abstract. Made it one person's wandering path, which also pays off the ending.
- **06:** the ending needed its cost visible. The price board is planted in shot 1 so $1,280 lands.
- **07:** two embarrassing bubbles were redundant. Kept one, and pivoted to the interviewer's bubble sooner.
- **08:** the "next break" twist needed a picture, not only text. Added the city tied to lamp posts the next morning.
- **09:** the adult showing a promotion email was too on-the-nose. Kept it, but at 2 s; the box with the old cardboard rocket carries the turn.
- **10:** the original ending (a text saying "stay inside forever") was predictable. Replaced with the peephole reveal.
