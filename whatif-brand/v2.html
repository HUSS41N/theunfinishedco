How it's built

The studio pipeline for this look: flat shapes from Illustrator, rigged and animated in After Effects. Rebuilt as code: shapes generated as SVG, rigs as joint angles, animation as JSON keys.

Shapes

Flat, no outlines

Every part is a filled shape. Depth comes from a second, darker same-hue shape cut to fit inside the part (face shade, sleeve side, seat side), never from strokes or gradients.

Rig

Pivots at joints

Shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee and ankle, with ball caps so a raised or crossed limb leaves no gap. Reach targets solve by two-bone IK; the torso can bend and the head tilts at the neck.

Layers

Order is depth

Set back, actors behind, set mid, props, actors, props in front, set front, speech bubbles, screen text, then transitions. Every group is a named layer.

Motion

Smooth and layered

Spline keys, overlapping action, actions that blend in and out, a planted-foot walk, idle breathing and blinks, and seven shot transitions. Deterministic, so a frame never jitters.

Sources: the Illustrator → After Effects pipeline (DVXUser), anchors at joints (Duik rigging), rig hierarchy and layer order (Toon Boom), and flat shading by clipping (Shutterstock).

Character builder

Every option maps to one field of a cast entry. Start from one of the twelve palettes or set each colour; build women and girls with the woman and girl builds, ponytails, pigtails or long curls, a bow, earrings, lashes and lipstick. Then copy the JSON straight into a scene file.

    Faces & poses
    Mouths, eyes, brows, gaze and a three-quarter turn, then all 35 named poses: the originals plus hands on hips, arms crossed, reach up, facepalm, clap, dance, kneel, crouch, run, jump, sitting on the floor and lying down. Any angle can be written by hand.
    
      
    
      
    
    
    
    
    
      
    Generated sets
    Sixteen generated sets, each built from SVG layers: back (walls, sky, windows), mid (what an actor can stand behind) and front. New in v3: kitchen, bedroom, café, hospital, bus, rooftop, classroom and living room. Every set is wider than the frame, so the camera can pan.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Real scale
    Sets and props are drawn at real size for an actor at scale 1: a character stands 800 px tall (1.7 m), so 1 m ≈ 470 px. Frame a wide shot with camera zoom (down to 0.4), never by shrinking the actors. Every thumbnail above is zoom 0.5 with the actor at scale 1.

    The props
    Held props attach to a wrist and follow the forearm; scene props stand in the set, at real size. Any field can be keyframed: wall rows, trail progress, phone messages.
    
      
    
    
    
    
    
      
    Colour in threes
    Twelve character palettes, then every colour as a base with its derived shade and light. A cast entry with "palette": "berry" takes every colour it doesn't set itself from the palette; "auto" picks one from the cast id, so a crowd gets a stable mix. Select a card to copy its name.

    Motion loops
    Each loop is one do action on the same character, at the full 24 fps with idle life (breathing, weight shift, blinks) underneath.
    
      
    
    
    
    
    
      
    Smooth motion
    The v3 engine moves like hand-keyed animation: spline keys, overlapping action, actions that blend in and out, a walk whose feet stay planted, and real shot transitions. Nothing procedural is random from frame to frame and nothing oscillates above 5 Hz, so nothing jitters. Below: examples/motion-demo.json live in flat style, beside the MP4 the renderer made from the same file.

    keys
    Spline keys
    Keys form a monotone cubic spline: they ease in at the first key, pass through middle keys without stopping, and never overshoot. Dashed: the same keys with "ease": "linear".
    
        
    do
    Blended actions
    Every action with from/to mixes in and out over blend seconds (0.2 by default, 0.45 for wave and reach), so nothing snaps. Dashed: no blend.
    
        
    walk
    Planted feet
    Footsteps are planned in world space, so a standing foot never slides. Travel eases in over the first half step and out over the last; the cadence comes from the distance and stride; the body bobs once a step with a small head counter-bob.
    {"act": "walk", "from": 3.1, "to": 4.9, "dx": 520}

    Text & safe zones
    Every video posts to YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and TikTok, so text has to clear all three apps' buttons and captions, and stay up long enough to read with the sound off. The engine enforces both: it fits, wraps and clamps every line into the combined safe rect, and --check flags any text that leaves too soon.
    
      
    
        
        
    
          
    Reading time
    
          
    read = min(7, max(1.2, 0.375 × words, characters ÷ 17) + 0.5 with a new visual + 0.5 for a 3+ digit number)
    
          
          
    
            
            
          
    
          
    2.25 s
    
          
      
          
      
          
      The text must be readable for that long: at least 90% opaque and within 2% of its final size and place. The entry animation (6 frames here) does not count.
